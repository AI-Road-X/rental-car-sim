## Title
How to Sanity-Check an AI-Generated China Itinerary Before Booking

## Slug
check-ai-china-itinerary-route

## Excerpt
A practical workflow for checking the geographic logic of a multi-city China itinerary generated by ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or another AI.

## Body HTML
<p>AI can produce a China itinerary in seconds, but the route can still be inefficient. A polished day-by-day answer may quietly add unnecessary backtracking, stack several long transfers into too few days, or place cities in an awkward order.</p>

<p>Before you book trains, flights and hotels, it is worth doing a simple geographic sanity check.</p>

<h2>Start with the route, not the attraction list</h2>

<p>For a first-time China trip, the most important planning decision is often the order of the cities. Exact restaurants and attractions can be adjusted later. If the geographic skeleton is wrong, the whole trip becomes harder.</p>

<p>Take a route such as:</p>

<p><strong>Beijing → Xi’an → Chengdu → Shanghai</strong></p>

<p>In text, this looks reasonable. The next step is to put those stops on one map, estimate the rough travel burden, and check whether the sequence creates obvious detours.</p>

<h2>A practical AI itinerary checking workflow</h2>

<ol>
<li><strong>Ask your preferred AI to draft the trip.</strong> ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or another assistant is fine.</li>
<li><strong>Extract the major stops.</strong> Use overnight cities and major transfer stops rather than every restaurant or museum.</li>
<li><strong>Visualize the route.</strong> This makes long jumps and repeated returns easier to spot.</li>
<li><strong>Reorder the cities if necessary.</strong> Fix the geographic structure before optimizing the daily schedule.</li>
<li><strong>Send the correction back to AI.</strong> Ask it to rebuild the day-by-day itinerary around the cleaner route.</li>
<li><strong>Verify live details.</strong> Check current train schedules, flight times, visa rules, closures, tickets and opening hours with official sources before paying.</li>
</ol>

Paste the main stops from an itinerary and it will visualize the route, estimate rough travel load, flag possible backtracking, let you reorder or remove stops, and generate a correction prompt you can paste back into your AI assistant.</p>

<h2>What the checker can tell you</h2>

<ul>
<li>whether the route shape contains obvious detours;</li>
<li>whether one leg is much longer than the others;</li>
<li>whether the itinerary appears too transfer-heavy for the number of days;</li>
<li>whether a simple reordering reduces the geographic burden;</li>
<li>whether the route is clear enough to share with a travel partner.</li>
</ul>

<h2>What it cannot tell you</h2>

<p>A route-sanity tool is not a live timetable or travel agency. Straight-line distance and heuristic travel-load estimates do not replace real rail, road or flight data. They also do not know current visa policy, venue closures, weather or ticket inventory.</p>

<p>Use the checker to decide whether the <em>shape</em> of the trip makes sense. Then verify the details with official transport operators, government sources, venues and booking providers.</p>

<h2>Why this matters more in a large country</h2>

<p>China’s major destinations are spread across a very large area, and two cities that look like a simple addition to a text itinerary can represent a meaningful transfer. The more cities you add, the more important the route order becomes.</p>

<p>A good workflow is therefore:</p>

<p><strong>AI draft → map check → route correction → live schedule check → booking.</strong></p>

<p>If you already have a China itinerary from an AI assistant, blog, spreadsheet or friend, you can paste the main stops into <a href="https://routeriff.vercel.app/?src=mattchinaguide-bottom">RouteRiff</a> and check the route before you start booking.</p>
