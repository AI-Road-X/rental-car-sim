This is a coherent one-direction route. If your flights work better in the opposite direction, Venice → Florence → Rome is equally sensible geographically.

Pacing pressure

Seven calendar days are not seven full sightseeing days when you include arrival, departure, packing, checkout, station access, the train itself, arrival transport and check-in.

When seven days can work

  • You accept a highlights-first trip rather than trying to see every major attraction.
  • Your arrival and departure times do not erase two full days.
  • You stay close enough to useful transport that transfer days remain manageable.
  • You resist adding a fourth overnight city.

When it starts to feel too fast

The trip becomes more fragile when seven days includes two long-haul flight days, you have fixed timed attractions in every city, or you add side trips that create extra early starts and luggage moves.

What should you cut first?

Do not automatically cut the city with the shortest train ride. Cut based on what you actually want from Italy. If the goal is ancient Rome plus Renaissance Florence, Venice may be the optional third stop. If Venice is a non-negotiable bucket-list city, keep it and reduce the attraction count elsewhere.
