Tokyo → Hakone → Kyoto → Osaka: which order makes sense?

All four stops are popular. The mistake is treating the list as if the order does not matter. If you start around Tokyo and finish in Kansai, Hakone usually belongs before Kyoto, not after it.

If your flights are reversed

If you arrive in Kansai and leave from Tokyo, reverse the logic and test Osaka → Kyoto → Hakone → Tokyo. Your arrival and departure airports should shape the city order before you book the middle of the trip.

Is four stops in seven days too rushed?

It can be. Four bases means several hotel changes, and a transfer costs more than the scheduled train time: packing, checkout, station access, waiting, arrival transport and check-in all consume usable time.

RouteRiff can use the trip-day count as pacing context, but it does not know your live train or hotel details. Use the route check to identify the pressure points, then verify the actual schedule.

Should Hakone be a day trip instead?

That depends on why you are going. If the ryokan or onsen stay is one of the trip highlights, another hotel change may be worth it. If Hakone is only a quick sightseeing stop, compare the overnight version with a day-trip version before committing.

What it does not replace

RouteRiff is not a live Shinkansen planner. It does not verify exact train times, JR Pass value, luggage-forwarding rules, attraction hours, weather, prices or ticket inventory. Verify those details with official transport and booking sources before paying.
