What RouteRiff looks for
  • rough travel load across the route;
  • very long jumps;
  • obvious backtracking;
  • the biggest detour stop;
  • average estimated travel load per day when you provide a trip length.

The trip-day count matters

Enter the number of days when you build the route. RouteRiff uses that as pacing context. A route that is plausible over fourteen days may deserve a warning over five.

Hotel changes have hidden costs

Transfer burden includes more than the ride itself: packing, checkout, station or airport time, waiting, local transport and check-in all consume usable travel time. RouteRiff does not model every one of those details, so its travel-load estimate is deliberately directional rather than timetable-grade.

Do not optimize away the trip you want

A detour is not automatically bad. You may keep one for a scenic train, festival, friend or bucket-list destination. The useful outcome is knowing the cost before you lock in non-refundable bookings.
